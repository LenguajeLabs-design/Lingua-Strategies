# Product Brief: Lingua Strategies

## One-sentence vision
A practical, research-informed field guide that helps teachers of multilingual learners know what to do tomorrow, understand why it works, and build expertise over time.

## Origin problem
Teachers can spend years piecing together multilingual-learner practice from scattered PD sessions, framework documents, books, conference notes, websites, colleagues, experiments, successes, and failures.

The problem is not that the field lacks good knowledge. The problem is that the knowledge is fragmented, often written for specialists, and rarely organized around the immediate questions a working teacher actually has.

A teacher should not need twenty years of trial and error to discover the handful of principles, routines, and teaching moves that could make tomorrow’s lesson better.

## The product we wish existed
A trustworthy place that effectively says:

**Start here.**

This will help.
This is what it looks like.
This is why it matters.
This is what you can try tomorrow.
And when you are ready, here is where to go deeper.

## Product shift
The original concept was a library of 52 modelable EAL strategies, particularly for co-teaching.

That library remains the MVP and practical heart of the project, but it now sits inside a larger concept:

**A guided field guide for becoming a stronger educator of multilingual learners.**

The product should curate, translate, connect, and organize knowledge from the field rather than simply accumulating resources.

## Core user needs
Users should be able to enter through authentic classroom problems, such as:
- My students do not participate in whole-class discussion.
- My multilingual learners cannot access this text.
- Students understand the topic but struggle to write about it.
- I need to introduce vocabulary without spending the whole lesson on vocabulary.
- I want to scaffold without making the work easier.
- I have a newcomer tomorrow. Where do I start?
- I want to use students’ home languages productively.
- I am an EAL specialist and want to contribute more intentionally inside the classroom.
- I am a classroom teacher and want to understand what my EAL colleague is doing.

The site should route these needs to practical strategies and deeper learning.

## Product promise
**Teachers should never leave a page knowing more but still not knowing what to do.**

Every useful page should contain an actionable next step.

A recurring CTA / content pattern should be:

### Try this tomorrow
A concrete, low-friction classroom action.

## Core information architecture
Long term, organize the product into five areas:

### Foundations
Understand the field without needing a graduate course first.

### Practical Toolbox
Search and browse classroom strategies by teacher need.

### Co-Teaching & Collaboration
Move EAL expertise out of the silo and into shared classroom practice.

### Reading, Writing & Academic Language
Develop deeper expertise in literacy and disciplinary language.

### Grow as a Multilingual Educator
Explore research, frameworks, resources, and professional-learning pathways.

## MVP scope
The MVP should make the larger vision visible while building only the most useful core.

### Build now
- Home
- “What are you trying to do?” need-based discovery
- Strategy Library
- Strategy detail view
- “Try this tomorrow” implementation view
- Lightweight About / Philosophy page
- Lightweight placeholders or preview cards for Foundations, Co-Teaching, Reading & Writing, and Grow as an Educator

### Do not build yet
- Authentication
- Database
- Teacher profiles
- School dashboards
- AI coach
- Analytics
- Paid plans
- Complex CMS
- Community features

## Home page concept
Primary message:

**Teach multilingual learners with more confidence.**

Supporting copy:
Practical, research-informed guidance for the classroom moments teachers actually face.

Primary entry point:

**What are you trying to do?**

Possible need cards:
- Build understanding before reading
- Get students talking
- Teach vocabulary
- Support writing
- Scaffold a complex task
- Check understanding
- Support a newcomer
- Use home languages
- Co-teach effectively
- Give useful feedback

Secondary entry points:
- Browse all strategies
- New to multilingual education? Start here.
- Co-teaching with an EAL specialist?

## Strategy experience
Each strategy should have two layers.

### Quick Version — about 2 minutes
- The problem it solves
- What the strategy is
- When to use it
- 3–5 steps
- Teacher script
- “Try this tomorrow” action

### Go Deeper — about 10 minutes
- Why it works
- What to notice in student responses
- Common mistakes
- Variations
- Subject / grade examples
- Co-teaching application
- Research / framework connection
- Further reading when available

## Co-teaching thread
Co-teaching remains an important differentiator and should be woven through the product rather than isolated as a separate app.

Use the collaboration cycle:

**Plan → Model → Notice → Reflect → Try**

For relevant strategies, show:
- how an EAL specialist might model it in 5–10 minutes
- what the classroom teacher should watch for
- a 2–5 minute debrief question
- how the classroom teacher could reuse it independently

## MVP user stories
- As a classroom teacher, I can describe what I am trying to accomplish and find practical strategies quickly.
- As a new EAL teacher, I can see what strong practice looks like without having to know all the terminology first.
- As an EAL specialist, I can find a strategy that fits a short push-in or demonstration window.
- As a co-teaching pair, we can identify one thing to model, notice, reflect on, and try again.
- As a teacher who wants deeper expertise, I can move from a quick classroom move into a clearer explanation of the underlying principle.
- As a busy teacher, I can leave the site with something useful to try tomorrow.

## Initial strategy filters
- Teacher need: comprehension, participation, vocabulary, writing, scaffolding, assessment, independence, multilingual assets, collaboration
- Focus: Comprehension, Speaking, Reading, Writing, Independence, Multilingual Assets
- Time: 5 min, 10 min, 20+ min
- Domain: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing
- Lesson phase: Before, During, After
- Co-teaching use: Lead a mini-lesson, Shared facilitation, Confer/circulate, Observe/reflect

## First milestone
Build the architecture with all 52 seed strategies visible, but deeply polish only 10 strategy pages first.

Suggested first 10:
1. Preview key vocabulary with visuals
2. Build background knowledge before reading
3. Language objective + content objective
4. Think-Pair-Share with sentence stems
5. Intentional wait time
6. Chunk teacher directions
7. Oral rehearsal before writing
8. Deconstruct a mentor text
9. Build a shared word bank
10. Gradually remove scaffolds

## Success test
The first version succeeds if a teacher can open it, identify a real classroom need, find a relevant strategy in under two minutes, and say:

**“I can try that tomorrow.”**

A stronger success signal is when the same teacher returns later to understand why the move worked and explore the field more deeply.
